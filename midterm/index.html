mid-term learning diary

Joyce（Li Jiao）

  1. 1

    Teamwork · I Could Speak Up Before I Had an Idea

    Early in the project, my teammates could spend over an hour bouncing ideas off each other. One would make a suggestion, and the other would challenge it and offer something new. I mostly listened. When they asked what I thought, I often said, “Nothing yet.” I was still figuring out the brief, and I didn’t know them well enough to feel comfortable interrupting. Looking back, I could have said what was actually on my mind: I wasn’t sure we were all trying to solve the same problem. Next time, I want to say where I’m stuck while the ideas are still on the table. That gives the team a chance to work through the uncertainty together.

  2. 2

    Design Research · Mapping Changed What I Looked For

    In my first interview at Yusof Ishak House, what stuck with me was how hot the student found the building. I held on to that answer so tightly that I started thinking about cooling before I had a clear research question. Spending an afternoon walking through the building changed that. As we mapped the rooms on Level 4, I began to see the gap between what the building offered and what people could actually use. A student outside might be unsure whether they could enter an air-conditioned room. The Guitar Club could get a booking and still have to split practice into groups to make the room work. Putting those experiences alongside the map helped me see that comfort was one part of a larger question about which spaces people could find, enter, and use for what they came to do.

  3. 3

    Research Bias · I Was Too Quick to Explain Away an Exception

    Several of our early interviewees had backgrounds similar to ours. When one Chinese student said she liked the building, we put it down to her having studied in the United States. I accepted that explanation without asking what she liked about it. Later, a German exchange student told us he preferred the open-air seating because he got cold in air-conditioned rooms. His practical concern was finding a working power outlet. That made me notice how differently I had treated the earlier answers. I pursued the complaint about heat and explained away the response that challenged it. In the next phase, I want to interview people with different reasons for being there, including those who choose to return and those who usually go elsewhere. When an answer surprises me, I want to ask one more question before settling on my own explanation.
